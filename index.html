Reference: SD rules, main connectives, and subderivation scope

Inspect the main connective

In A ⊃ (B & C), the main connective is ⊃. In A & (B ⊃ C), the main connective is &.

Conjunction Elimination (&E)

From an accessible conjunction P & Q, infer P or Q—a whole conjunct. You cannot skip into a conjunct’s internal structure in one step.

Conditional Elimination (⊃E)

From an accessible P ⊃ Q and P, infer the entire consequent Q.

Conditional Introduction (⊃I)

Assume P in a subderivation, derive Q within it, then end that subderivation and assert P ⊃ Q at the parent indentation level. Cite the full subderivation range.

Nested subderivations

After closing an inner subderivation, return exactly one indentation level left. After closing the outer subderivation, return left again.

Negation Introduction (~I)

To prove ~P, assume P and derive a sentence and its negation within that subderivation. Close it and infer ~P outside.

Negation Elimination (~E)

To prove P, assume ~P and derive a sentence and its negation within that subderivation. Close it and infer P outside.

Biconditional Introduction (≡I)

Complete two subderivations, one from the left side to the right side and one in the reverse direction. Cite both ranges to establish the biconditional.

Disjunction Elimination (∨E)

From P ∨ Q, open one subderivation assuming P and another assuming Q; derive the same target in both, then cite the disjunction and both ranges.

Accessible lines

Outside a finished subderivation, its interior lines cannot be used directly. You can cite a properly completed subderivation as a whole for a rule that requires it.

These five rules require subderivations in basic SD: ⊃I, ~I, ~E, ≡I, and ∨E. The rule is applied after the relevant subderivation(s) have ended, at the enclosing indentation level.
